Understanding the Human Heart

The heart is a hollow muscular organ, roughly the size of a closed fist. It sits in the mediastinum, the middle compartment of the chest, between the lungs and tilted slightly to the left.

It works as a double pump. The right side sends oxygen-poor blood to the lungs (pulmonary circulation). The left side sends oxygen-rich blood to the rest of the body (systemic circulation). Each heartbeat is a coordinated squeeze that keeps blood moving in one direction.

External Anatomy

Apex

The pointed lower tip, formed by the left ventricle.

Base

The back surface where the big vessels attach; mostly the left atrium.

Coronary sulcus

A groove between the atria and ventricles that holds coronary vessels.

Interventricular sulci

Front and back grooves marking the boundary between the two ventricles.

Four Chambers. One Coordinated Pump.

Right atrium

Receives oxygen-poor blood from the body through the superior and inferior venae cavae.

Right ventricle

Pumps oxygen-poor blood through the pulmonary valve to the lungs.

Left atrium

Receives oxygen-rich blood from the lungs through the pulmonary veins.

Left ventricle

Pumps oxygen-rich blood through the aortic valve into the aorta.

Valves do not contract. Blood pressure pushes them open going forward, and pushes them shut if blood tries to flow backward.

Arteries carry blood away from the heart and veins carry it toward the heart. That is why pulmonary arteries carry oxygen-poor blood and pulmonary veins carry oxygen-rich blood. (Red and blue are only teaching colours; blood is never blue.)

The Electrical System Behind Every Beat

  1. The sinoatrial (SA) node normally starts each beat.
  2. The signal spreads across both atria, which contract.
  3. The atrioventricular (AV) node slows the signal briefly so the ventricles can fill.
  4. The signal travels down the bundle of His.
  5. It splits into the right and left bundle branches.
  6. Purkinje fibres spread it quickly through the ventricles, which contract.

An ECG records this electrical activity (not the force of the heartbeat). The P wave is the atria activating, the QRS complex is the ventricles activating, and the T wave is the ventricles recovering.
